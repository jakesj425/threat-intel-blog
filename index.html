🔥 DFIR Wild Powder: This Week's Threats That Would Make Your Jaw Drop 🔍

🔗 Thing #1: macOS.Gaslight is doing something I've never seen before. A DPRK-aligned Rust backdoor that turns prompt injection on the security analyst — not the sandbox. It literally tries to manipulate you during analysis. From a forensic angle, this is bonkers: the malware is weaponizing the investigator's own workflow against them. If you're doing malware analysis on this thing and your tool starts acting weird... that's by design.

🔗 Thing #2: GhostShell is targeting Ukraine's UAV supply chain with Besomar-themed lures. We're seeing a deliberate supply chain compromise aimed at drone warfare infrastructure. The forensic artifacts here are going to be gold — look for compromised firmware update chains and weaponized documents targeting defense industrial base. This is the kind of campaign that keeps you up at night.

🔗 Thing #3: StrikeShark drops a custom SharkLoader + Cobalt Strike Beacon combo. What's interesting from a DFIR perspective is the multi-stage delivery — SharkLoader is doing the initial recon and staging before handing off to Cobalt Strike. If you're hunting for this, look for the loader's C2 patterns before the Cobalt Strike beacon phones home. The IOC window is wider than most people think.

🔗 Thing #4: The FortiBleed credential factory is still spinning. Inside FortiBleed reverse-engineered a CyberStrike harvester running a global FortiGate credential factory. The scary part? It's been going since at least 2024 and it's still active. If your org has FortiGate appliances and you haven't rotated credentials + checked for config dumps... today's the day.

🔗 Thing #5: AI supply chain attacks are here — and they're on npm/PyPi. The Skill Marketplace report shows threat actors poisoning open-source AI toolkits. For DFIR folks: this means your next incident might start with a compromised `pip install`. Check your dependency trees. The attack surface is massive.

The big picture? The stacks are getting deeper. Social engineering + supply chain abuse + better evasion, all in one campaign. It's no longer enough to patch CVEs — you have to assume the build pipeline itself is compromised.

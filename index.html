Opinion of a DFIR Guy

🔥 Why Your EDR Killing Playbook Is Already Obsolete — Three Reports Explain Today 🔍

🔗 Thing #1: Zero-day BYOVD is now a ransomware standard play. The Gentlemen didn't just find an exploit — they killed Palo Alto Cortex XDR, SentinelOne, ESET, and Windows Defender via `ktapi.sys` from Kontron (a legit driver nobody had blocklisted). The exploit dodges SMEP/SMAP like it's 2008. For DFIR: check for unsigned third-party drivers in memory, and treat driver blocklists like candy — they're always incomplete. Source

🔗 Thing #2: ValleyRAT's process injection is getting creative with Donut shellcode. SilverFox is dropping 31 MD5s and 36 SHA256s across fake installers targeting both Chinese and Japanese victims. Pool Party Variant 7 injection + DLL sideloading + junk code insertion. For DFIR: hunt `regsvr32.exe` spawning from `install.exe` processes, and check `HKCU\Software\Classes\CLSID` for new COM hijack persistence. Source

🔗 Thing #3: Supply chain hits npm — 45 malicious packages in one shot. Miasma Mini Shai-Hulud doesn't just go after developers — it goes after CI/CD. Backstage plugins and GitHub Actions both compromised. That's your build pipeline, production, and every downstream customer in one swing. For DFIR: verify every SHA256 in `package-lock.json` against registry manifests — the difference is the artifact that shows compromise. Source

🔗 Thing #4: BTMOB Android RAT is MaaS for $5K with regional phishing templates. Evolved from SpySolr, uses Accessibility Services to escalate, targets Argentina/Brazil tax agencies. Steal screenshots, SMS, device activity — then wipe `logcat` clean. For DFIR: look for `android.permission.BIND_ACCESSIBILITY_SERVICE` apps with no Play Store listing but network connections to `aliyuncs.com` or DuckDNS domains. Source

🔗 Thing #5: Defence Impairment Olympics shows how persistence beats detection. One stego webshell on ColdFusion led to timestomping, WMI Event Consumers clearing logs, killing Sysmon/Filebeat, uninstalling ModSecurity. The forensic artifact trail goes cold when they kill the logging tools first. For DFIR: check `HKLM\SOFTWARE\Microsoft\Windows NT\CurrentVersion\Winlogon\Notify` for WMI persistence artifacts — that registry key survives reboots even when Event Logs get nuked. Source

The big picture? Kill-chain thinking is dead. Attackers are now exploiting trust chains — legitimate drivers, accessibility frameworks, build pipelines, and logging systems. The attack surface isn't the malware — it's the assumptions about what's trusted.
